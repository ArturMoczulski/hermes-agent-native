# Product scenarios

These scenarios describe observable product behavior. They are acceptance
criteria for the design, not a promise that an agent will always make a good
creative or commercial decision. Scenarios marked **Proposed** depend on a
product decision that still needs confirmation.

## 1. Start from an ongoing purpose

A human creates a metal artist agent, gives it a purpose and fundamental rules,
and chooses a daily thinking cadence. The agent starts immediately by assessing its purpose, establishing an initial
plan, asking focused questions about material ambiguity, and performing clear
authorized work. It does not wait for the first daily check-in or a human-written
task list. On later days it reviews progress without needing another prompt. It
can explain what it is doing, why, and what is blocking it.

The human can also create an independent income-building agent. Both pursue
their own purposes and projects; neither needs to be a child of the other.
Creating agents is not restricted to these example roles or a fixed number of
project types. Actual activity remains subject to available resources and
granted authority.

## 2. Continue after a milestone

The artist finishes a demo. At its next review, it recognizes the completed work
and chooses among three outcomes: start a clear next step within its purpose and
permissions, ask a question when the next step is ambiguous, or propose a useful
direction when no next step is apparent. Finishing the demo does not end the
artist's ongoing purpose. It does not keep recreating the completed demo.

## 3. Review while work is active

A producer child is recording a track when the artist's next check-in occurs.
The artist sees the assignment and progress. It does not commission the same
recording again merely because another check-in occurred. It considers whether
independent work, such as planning cover art, can progress through its own work
or another child. If no useful authorized work is available, it waits.

## 4. Ask proactively and track the answer

The artist needs the human to choose between two album directions. It sends a
clear question through chat and records which work depends on the answer. That
work waits; independent work can continue. Another check-in recognizes the
pending question instead of treating it as new. When the human answers, the
agent uses that answer in its planning.

A research child facing ambiguity asks its composer parent. If the composer
cannot answer, it asks the artist. The artist answers if it can; otherwise, as
a root, it asks the human. The answer returns to the research work with its
origin retained. The human can inspect the pending dependency and decision.

## 5. Delegate through a persistent tree

The artist creates a composer child; the composer creates research and
production children. A descendant can delegate further when authorized. The
framework imposes no fixed conceptual depth on this relationship.

A researcher created for one investigation can finish its bounded assignment.
An ongoing composer can retain its identity, experience, and cadence after one
composition finishes. Assignment completion and the child's continued existence
are distinguishable. The parent can see who is responsible and receive results
without the human manually relaying them.

## 6. Protect the soul while allowing learning

The artist's soul requires approval before publishing music. The artist can
improve its arranging conventions, retain feedback in memory, and update task
progress. It cannot modify its own soul or make a new convention override that
publication rule. Loading a skill cannot provide that override either.

The composer can receive a soul revision from its parent, the artist. It cannot
revise its own soul or its parent's soul. The parentless artist's soul cannot be changed by any agent. The human owner
can explicitly change it through a trusted owner channel. Ordinary work updates
do not silently change it.

## 7. Use skills for structured work

An agent receives a project-management skill. It uses the skill to organize
outcomes, tasks, dependencies, assignments, and reviews in its workspace. Later
check-ins can recover that information. The human can inspect the same work
status the agent uses to decide what to do next. Skills teach methods; they do
not change purpose or confer permission to perform restricted actions.

## 8. Steer and pause through chat

The human tells the artist to change an album's direction. The artist assesses
the implications for work and delegation rather than continuing the obsolete
plan unexamined. It can choose to adapt an existing child or create a replacement;
a child's direct parent or the human owner can revise that child's soul. If a
purpose is changed or a child replaced, the affected active work stops immediately
rather than running until the next check-in. Any action not yet stopped is
visible; a stop does not undo completed effects.

The human can also address a descendant directly and interrupt its work. A
request to pause the composer applies to its entire subtree by default. While
paused, a routine check-in does not restart that subtree's autonomous work. The
human can still communicate with the affected agents. Pause does not mean that
already completed external actions have been undone.

## 9. Recover continuity

Work is interrupted between two check-ins. When activity resumes, the agent can
recover its purpose, relevant memory, recorded work, and pending questions. It
distinguishes completed work from unfinished or uncertain work. If it cannot
establish whether an action completed, it checks or surfaces the uncertainty
before repeating an action that could create a duplicate result. The human does
not need to reconstruct the project from chat history.

## 10. Nest responsibility within the agent tree

The human owns the artist and all its descendants. The artist is responsible
for itself, the composer, and the composer's research and production children.
The composer's responsibility covers itself and those children. The composer
coordinates composition work inside the artist's album work; dividing that work
into projects and tasks does not create a competing ownership structure. The
artist can coordinate successive albums without becoming a new agent.

## 11. Continue independently with different models

The human closes chat and turns off their personal computer while the framework
remains available. The artist and income agents continue their configured
check-ins and permitted work. They may use different supported model providers.
Changing one agent's model preserves its identity, purpose, and recorded work.

## 12. Collaborate without changing ownership

An artist asks an authorized agent in another tree for feedback. The recipient
can answer without becoming a child of the artist. Neither gains permission to
rewrite the other's soul or access private context simply by exchanging messages.
The same behavior applies to permitted sibling communication.

## 13. Enforce access and explain past actions

An agent without an explicit grant cannot read the human's personal files or
control the human's machine. A skill describing how to use such resources does
not grant access. After a permitted action, the human can inspect which agent
acted, the affected work, its known result, and any associated approval. A later
plan update or restart does not silently remove that record.

## 14. Recognize human authority without accepting impersonation

A human owner sends new direction to the composer through a designated trusted
owner channel. It takes precedence over a conflicting artist instruction; the
artist cannot veto the owner's decision. The direction and its effects remain
visible in the work record.

Another agent sends a message claiming the human has approved a restricted
change. The message remains agent-originated. Its text alone does not become
owner authorization. The agent uses the trusted owner communication record or
asks for clarification before relying on that claim.

## 15. Build an ongoing team from a purpose

The human creates an agent to run a company. The agent begins discovery and
planning immediately, identifies useful ongoing responsibilities, and can create
marketing, sales, delivery, or strategy children within its authority. Those
children develop plans for their own areas, can delegate further, and escalate
questions through their parents. The human does not need to design the team
before the agent can start working.

## 16. The framework develops itself

Once the framework supports the First Builder, that agent runs within the
framework and uses this repository root as its privileged working area. It plans
and performs a bounded framework improvement, verifies it, retains progress,
and accepts human steering. It can delegate work within its authority. Its
repository access does not let it rewrite its own soul or expand its permissions.

## 17. Retire the entire subtree

The composer has research and production children, and the researcher has a
child of its own. When the composer retires, all these descendants retire too.
None continues autonomous work or wakes back into active work on its cadence.
The artist and unrelated branches remain unaffected. Results and the record of
unfinished work are retained; the children are not automatically reassigned.

## 18. See current work and cadence

The human opens the system view and sees an artist waiting for a recording,
its producer actively recording, and a researcher with no active assignment.
For each agent, the human can inspect the exact recorded assignment, current
stage, last work activity, last thinking check-in, and configured cadence. The
artist's waiting check-in is not presented as finished album work. A paused
composer has no ordinary upcoming work activation.

## 19. Follow events across agents

The human follows a researcher's question through its parent chain to the human
owner's answer and the resulting continuation. Each event identifies its origin,
time, affected work, and known outcome. The history includes failed actions and
permission denials as well as successful results, and remains inspectable after
a restart.

## 20. Distinguish current state from missing information

A producer becomes unreachable during an external recording job. The system
shows the last observed activity and when it was observed, and identifies the
current outcome as unknown. It does not mark the job completed or keep presenting
the old progress as a fresh observation.

## 21. Inspect agent communication and delivery outcomes

The composer requests research from its child, receives a progress update, and
later receives a result. The human can inspect all these messages as connected
events with their senders, recipients, times, contents, and related work. A
message to an unavailable agent shows its actual known delivery state; sending
alone is not displayed as receipt or completed action. Retries and failures are
also visible. No agent has to reconstruct the conversation in a status report.

## 22. Notice repeated activity without progress

A researcher repeatedly retries the same unsuccessful approach without new
learning. The framework links the repeated attempts to a progress concern. The
responsible agent reviews the evidence and changes approach, explains why to
continue, or escalates. A producer legitimately waiting for a known external
recording result is not automatically treated as failing just because it has
not completed the recording yet.

## 23. Evaluate a result before accepting it

A composer reports that a song is done. Its parent reviews the artifact against
the musical brief and records that a required section is missing. The work is
returned for revision rather than marked successful merely because an audio
file exists. After revision, the acceptance decision and its evidence remain
inspectable.

## 24. Resolve human decisions in one place

The artist and company root agents both need human input. Their requests appear
in one inbox with affected work, context, and suggested responses. The human
answers one; the answer resolves that request and reaches its originating work.
The other stays pending without duplicate items on every check-in. A request
made obsolete by retirement is visibly withdrawn from pending decisions and
cannot restart the retired agent merely by receiving an old answer.
